HOW I INTERPRET THE COMPARISON
I would keep BM3D as the main classical baseline for this experiment. Its mean
PSNR is 1.621 dB above NLM and its mean SSIM is higher too.
NLM has a lower median runtime: 0.515 seconds per crop versus
16.821 for BM3D. On this run, BM3D took about
32.7 times as long by the median timing measure.
That comparison describes this CPU run, not every possible machine.
The timing starts after image loading and excludes metric calculation and Drive writes.

The first displayed crop was selected by manifest order, not by its score.
In that crop, BM3D keeps more of the fine texture than NLM, which smooths it
strongly. The individual-crop numbers in the image are not the table averages.
I would not use one example alone to claim that a method wins on every image.

WHY THE DATA IS SPLIT BY SCENE
Several crops and captures can come from the same physical scene. If I treated
those crops as unrelated and randomly mixed them between splits, similar
content could appear in both training and validation. Keeping each scene in
one split makes the comparison less affected by this leakage.
The fixed split contains 364 training, 120 validation and 156 test crops.

WHERE THE CLEAN IMAGES ARE USED
The clean validation image is used to calculate PSNR and SSIM after filtering.
BM3D and NLM receive the noisy image, not the clean reference. The noisy-only
training manifest has no clean-target paths. This baseline experiment does not
train the neural network; clean references are evaluation targets here.

WHY THE COUNTS AND NUMBERS DIFFER BETWEEN M1 AND M2
The 24-crop validation subset was used for the earlier parameter calibration.
The 120-crop set is the full M2 validation comparison. It includes that smaller
calibration subset, so it should not be called an independent held-out test.
The 156 test crops are a different split. I should not change a validation
curve label to a test mean just to make two figures look the same.
The selected filter settings stay fixed: BM3D sigma_psd = 0.20 and NLM h = 0.08.

The historical M2 BM3D mean is 38.148482767570 dB, while my recorded
Colab mean is 38.148500558175 dB. Their full-precision difference is
0.000017791 dB. They display as 38.148 and 38.149 at three
decimal places because they sit on opposite sides of a rounding boundary.
This is not evidence of a new tuning improvement. The historical M1 test
number, 38.440 dB, describes a different recorded evaluation.
